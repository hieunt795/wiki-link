## Scale of Insurer Participation
[LLM] Fed Chicago working paper data: US life insurers increased private placement lending from $386bn (2014) to $849bn (2024), reaching 14% of life insurer general account assets. Growth explicitly linked to post-crisis low-rate environment.

## Structural Logic
[LLM] Life insurance liabilities are long-duration and illiquid -> private credit (long-dated, illiquid, floating spread) is natural asset-liability match. Post-2008 low rates compressed public fixed income spreads -> insurers moved into private credit for yield without visible public-market volatility (mark-to-model vs mark-to-market).

## PE Entry into Insurance as Capital Source
[LLM] Private equity firms acquired stakes in or founded life insurance companies (Athene/Apollo, Global Atlantic/KKR etc.) to access 'permanent capital' - long-dated, stable insurance liabilities that can fund illiquid private credit strategies at scale. This created a vertically integrated origination-to-funding loop.

## Governance Risks (IAIS 2025)
[LLM] International Association of Insurance Supervisors flagged: private credit creates credit risk, liquidity constraints, valuation uncertainty, and hidden leverage in insurer portfolios that require strong governance and supervisory oversight. Semi-liquid wrappers held by insurers create potential mismatch if redemption requests exceed quarterly liquidity caps.
