## Mechanism of the Scissors Effect

The "scissors effect" describes a regime shift where the central bank moves from being a net buyer of sovereign debt to a net seller (or passive roll-off holder) at the same time fiscal authorities increase their borrowing needs [RAW-CLIP].

### The Regime Shift (Case Study: Germany 2020-2025)

The effect is clearly visible in the German sovereign debt data [RAW-CLIP]:

- **2020-2021 (The Buffer Regime)**: The ECB was an active absorber of German debt under the PSPP, purchasing €52-55bn annually. This covered 25-40% of Germany's net new borrowing. The central bank acted as a stabilizer, financing a substantial portion of the pandemic fiscal response [RAW-CLIP].
- **2023-2024 (The Scissors Regime)**: The relationship inverted. The ECB began active portfolio reduction (QT). In 2023, the ECB withdrew €51.7bn net while Germany's net borrowing was only €27.2bn. In 2024, the ECB withdrawal (€72.2bn) was more than twice Germany's net issuance (€33.3bn) [RAW-CLIP].
- **2025-2026 (The Perfect Storm)**: As of early 2026, German net borrowing is rising again (estimated €67bn in 2025) while the ECB continues to allow its portfolio to run off. The simultaneous rise in supply and withdrawal of support forces the market to find alternative buyers for both new debt and the bonds the ECB no longer holds [RAW-CLIP].

## Implications for Market Stability

The scissors effect forces a reliance on alternative buyers, primarily Non-Bank Financial Intermediaries (NBFIs). Because these buyers operate with shorter investment horizons and more fragile funding structures than the central bank, the market becomes:
- **More Sensitive to Triggers**: Idiosyncratic fiscal news or trade policy shifts can cause sharp volatility spikes (e.g., the 30bp Bund move in March 2025) [RAW-CLIP].
- **Liquidity Dependent**: The market relies heavily on the repo market to finance leveraged buyers (hedge funds), making it vulnerable to funding stress [RAW-CLIP].

## Summary

The scissors effect is a structural challenge for sovereign debt markets transitioning away from decade-long central bank support. It intensifies the need for stable, long-term private sector buyers to fill a gap that is growing in two directions simultaneously. [LLM]
