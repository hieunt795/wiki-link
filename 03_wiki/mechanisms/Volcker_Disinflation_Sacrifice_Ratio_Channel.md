## Slack Channel
[RAW-BOOK Ch.19] Watts and Wray state that most economists accept disinflation will be accompanied by lower output and higher unemployment because temporary slack is needed to break inflationary expectations.

[RAW-BOOK Ch.19] They state that cumulative real costs from discretionary disinflation are substantial.

[LLM] This mechanism is the real-side counterpart to Volcker's nominal credibility shift.

[LLM] The Fed can raise real rates to stop inflation persistence, but the channel operates by weakening spending, labor bargaining, investment, and output.

## Sacrifice Ratio
[RAW-BOOK Ch.19] Watts and Wray define the sacrifice ratio as accumulated output loss during a disinflation episode, expressed as a percent of initial output, divided by the reduction in the inflation rate.

[RAW-BOOK Ch.19] They give the example that a sacrifice ratio of two means a one-point reduction in trend inflation is associated with cumulative GDP loss equal to two percent of initial output.

[LLM] The sacrifice ratio makes the Volcker choice measurable: credibility is bought by accepting output losses.

## Persistence and Hysteresis
[RAW-BOOK Ch.19] Watts and Wray distinguish persistence, where actual output remains below potential after disinflation ends, from hysteresis, where potential output itself is permanently lowered.

[RAW-BOOK Ch.19] They say hysteresis can arise when low confidence reduces investment and limits long-run output growth.

[LLM] This is why disinflation costs are not limited to the recession window.

[LLM] If investment and labor-market attachment are damaged, the economy can exit the disinflation with lower productive capacity.

## Link to Volcker
[RAW-BOOK Ch.7] Warjiyo and Juhro show that the post-1979 Fed reaction function raised nominal rates more than one-for-one against inflation.

[RAW-BOOK Ch.7] They also say the 1979-1982 monetary-targeting period produced larger interest-rate shocks.

[LLM] The Volcker channel can therefore be summarized as: inflation shock -> stronger Fed reaction function -> higher real rates -> demand compression -> unemployment/output losses -> weaker inflation persistence.

[LLM] This mechanism solved the credibility problem but transferred part of the adjustment burden onto employment, output, and investment.
