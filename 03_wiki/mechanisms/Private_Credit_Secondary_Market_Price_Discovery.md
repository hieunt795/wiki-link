## Definition
[LLM] Private credit secondary market: LP interests in private credit funds are traded between buyers and sellers after initial commitment. Unlike primary markets (new fund subscriptions), secondaries transfer existing interests at observable market prices.

## Size and Growth
[LLM] Global credit secondary volume (Evercore/Jefferies data):
- 2023: ~bn
- 2024: ~1bn
- 2025 estimated: >8bn
GP-led transactions rapidly becoming majority of deal volume. Senior secured direct lending continuation vehicles consistently price mid-90s to par.

## NAV Pricing Data (H1 2025, Jefferies)
[LLM] Average LP secondary pricing:
- All strategies: ~90% of NAV
- Credit secondaries: ~92% of NAV
- Senior direct lending: strong demand, near par
- Distressed/mezzanine/opportunistic: steeper discounts

## Price Discovery Function
[LLM] Secondaries impose observable market prices on otherwise marked-to-model assets. This creates a feedback loop absent in purely bilateral markets: secondary discounts to NAV become external benchmarks that stress the model-valuation narrative. Key implication: as secondaries scale, private credit loses the ability to remain purely mark-to-manager during stress events.

## Regime Shift
[LLM] Private credit ETFs (BIS analysis): if private credit ETFs grow, they introduce intraday price signals even for underlying illiquid assets, making mark-to-market pressure more immediate. IMF warns that rising retail participation via semi-liquid vehicles increases run risk and first-mover advantages.
