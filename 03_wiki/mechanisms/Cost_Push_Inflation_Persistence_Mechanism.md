## Mechanism
[RAW-BOOK §Inflation] The IMF text defines inflation as a sustained and persistent increase in the overall price level, not merely a relative-price change.

[RAW-BOOK §Inflation] It says a change in the relative price of a key good, such as oil, can initiate a process that leads to generalized inflation.

[RAW-BOOK §Inflation] It also states that appropriate policy actions can keep that initial price shock from worsening.

[LLM] The persistence mechanism is therefore a conversion problem: a one-time relative-price shock becomes a continuing increase in the aggregate price level.

## Cost Channel
[RAW-BOOK §Analyzing Inflation] The IMF text says cost-push inflation is caused by rising costs and may develop even when unemployment is high and resource utilization is low.

[RAW-BOOK §Analyzing Inflation] It identifies wages as frequently the most important cost of production.

[RAW-BOOK §Analyzing Inflation] It says wages rising out of line with productivity can initiate the inflation process.

[LLM] In stagflation, that matters because the economy can have weak output at the same time that firms face higher unit labor, energy, import, or intermediate-input costs.

## Monetary Accommodation
[RAW-BOOK §Inflation] The IMF text says a one-time increase in key goods can trigger chained price and wage increases if monetary policy is not sufficiently firm.

[RAW-BOOK §Analyzing Inflation] It says cost-push inflation cannot persist if monetary policy refuses to accommodate it.

[RAW-BOOK §Analyzing Inflation] It says that without accommodation, wage increases lead to higher unemployment rather than higher inflation.

[LLM] This is the hard stagflation tradeoff: refusing accommodation protects the nominal anchor but pushes more of the adjustment into output, unemployment, or real wages.

## Persistence Channels
| Channel | How persistence forms | Source basis |
|---|---|---|
| Wage-productivity mismatch | Wage growth exceeds productivity growth | [RAW-BOOK §Analyzing Inflation] |
| Energy/input shock | Key input prices trigger chained price increases | [RAW-BOOK §Inflation] |
| Inertial contracts | Past inflation becomes embedded in wage and financial contracts | [RAW-BOOK §Analyzing Inflation] |
| Policy accommodation | Monetary policy validates cost increases into aggregate inflation | [RAW-BOOK §Inflation] |

[LLM] A cost shock is therefore inflationary in levels immediately, but stagflationary in regime terms only when the economy cannot absorb the real-income loss without either falling activity or persistent nominal pass-through.
