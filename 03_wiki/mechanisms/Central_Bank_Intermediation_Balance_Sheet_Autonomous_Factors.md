Central bank intermediation describes the CB balance sheet expanding to substitute for failed interbank markets.

**Two modes (Bindseil Ch2):**

1. Relative intermediation: Some banks excess, others deficit. CB provides credit to deficit banks funded by deposits from surplus banks. Balance sheet does NOT expand. Normal functioning even with interbank stress (banks route via CB rather than each other).

2. Absolute intermediation: CB balance sheet EXPANDS. Surplus banks deposit with CB while deficit banks borrow. Total CB assets rise. Occurs when interbank market completely breaks down (stigma, counterparty risk, information asymmetry). Post-2008 ECB and 2020 Fed exhibited absolute intermediation at massive scale.

**Autonomous factors:** Items on the CB balance sheet outside direct CB control that affect bank reserves:
- Banknotes in circulation (drains reserves; seasonal patterns matter)
- Government deposits / TGA equivalent (drains reserves when government receives taxes)
- Net foreign assets
CBs must forecast autonomous factors to calibrate OMO volume for reserve neutrality.

**Collateral constraints as intermediation limit:**
When banks exhaust eligible collateral, CB cannot lend further without expanding collateral eligibility. Widening collateral framework (accepting lower-quality assets) extends absolute intermediation capacity. ECB used this extensively during European sovereign crisis.

**Leanness indicator:** Defined as the ratio of net central bank credit to total liquidity needs. A lean balance sheet (low intermediation) preserves market function; a bloated one (high absolute intermediation) entails moral hazard and central bank risk.
