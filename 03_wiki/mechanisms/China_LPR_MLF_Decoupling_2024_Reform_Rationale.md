## Problem With MLF-Anchored LPR

Pre-July 2024, the LPR was quoted as a spread over the MLF rate. Two structural defects:

1. **MLF is not market-formed**: PBC determined MLF via fixed-rate, quantity-bidding operations. This made MLF a policy decree, not a market price — so LPR inherited the same non-market character.
2. **Quote quality was low**: Quoting banks submitted spreads that did not accurately reflect the true financing costs of prime borrowers. [RAW-CLIP]

Result: LPR was nominally "market-based" but effectively administered — the 2015 interest rate liberalization was formal rather than substantive.

## 2024 Reform

- LPR decoupled from MLF rate
- LPR now anchors to the **7-day OMO reverse repo rate** (PBC's open market policy rate)
- Single short-term rate now transmits to both: credit market (via LPR → loan rates) and bond market (via yield curve)

## Significance

This reform aligns China with "mainstream countries'" monetary policy frameworks where a single short-term policy rate is the anchor. [RAW-CLIP — Guo 2025]

It also signals China's continued shift from quantity-based (M2, RRR) to price-based (interest rate) monetary policy — though the credit channel remains dominant in actual transmission. [LLM]
