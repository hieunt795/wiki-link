## Overview: Five Sections, 17 Principles

Published September 2008 by the Basel Committee on Banking Supervision (BCBS),
replacing the 2000 *Sound Practices for Managing Liquidity in Banking Organisations*.
Issued in response to the 2007–08 market turmoil that exposed critical failures in
liquidity risk management across the global banking system.
[RAW-BOOK bcbs144 para 2–4]

```
SECTION 1 — FUNDAMENTAL PRINCIPLE
  Principle 1  Liquidity risk management framework + HQLA cushion obligation

SECTION 2 — GOVERNANCE (Principles 2–4)
  Principle 2  Board sets liquidity risk tolerance
  Principle 3  Senior management: strategy, policies, board annual review
  Principle 4  Internal pricing: allocate liquidity costs/benefits/risks to business lines

SECTION 3 — MEASUREMENT AND MANAGEMENT (Principles 5–12)
  Principle 5  Cash flow projection framework (assets + liabilities + off-BS)
  Principle 6  Cross-entity, cross-currency, cross-business monitoring
  Principle 7  Funding diversification: sources, tenors, market access
  Principle 8  Intraday liquidity management
  Principle 9  Collateral management (encumbered vs unencumbered)
  Principle 10 Stress testing: institution-specific + market-wide scenarios
  Principle 11 Contingency Funding Plan (CFP)
  Principle 12 HQLA cushion: unencumbered, high-quality liquid assets

SECTION 4 — PUBLIC DISCLOSURE
  Principle 13 Quantitative + qualitative liquidity disclosure

SECTION 5 — SUPERVISORY ROLE (Principles 14–17)
  Principle 14 Comprehensive supervisory assessment
  Principle 15 Monitoring via internal reports, prudential reports, market data
  Principle 16 Supervisory intervention + remedial action authority
  Principle 17 Cross-border supervisory cooperation (home/host, CB)
[RAW-BOOK bcbs144 para 7]
```

## Principle 1 — Fundamental Standard

```
"A bank should establish a robust liquidity risk management framework that ensures
it maintains sufficient liquidity, including a cushion of unencumbered, high quality
liquid assets, to withstand a range of stress events, including those involving the
loss or impairment of both unsecured and secured funding sources."
[RAW-BOOK bcbs144 Principle 1]

Key elements:
  ① HQLA cushion: held against stress scenarios (unsecured + secured funding loss)
  ② Framework integration: embedded in bank-wide risk management
  ③ Supervisory backstop: supervisors assess adequacy; intervene if deficient
  ④ Not competitive pressure override: integrity of LRM not compromised by competition
  [RAW-BOOK bcbs144 para 8]
```

Scope: "medium and large complex banks" — but "broad applicability to all types."
Implementation proportional to size, business nature, complexity. [RAW-BOOK bcbs144 para 6]

## Governance Architecture (Principles 2–4)

```
BOARD OF DIRECTORS (ultimate responsibility):
  → Sets liquidity risk tolerance (P2)
  → Approves strategy, policies, practices annually (P3)
  → Receives regular reports on liquidity position
  → Immediate notification of new/emerging concerns
  [RAW-BOOK bcbs144 para 10, 18]

SENIOR MANAGEMENT (operational responsibility):
  → Develops and implements LRM strategy per risk tolerance (P3)
  → Allocates liquidity costs/benefits/risks to business lines via internal pricing (P4)
  → Monitors cross-entity, cross-currency positions (P6)
  → Reviews/updates CFP at least annually (P11)
  [RAW-BOOK bcbs144 para 11, 17, 19]

BUSINESS LINES:
  → Operate under approved policies, procedures, limits
  → Liquidity charge assigned to positions/portfolios/transactions
  → Incentives aligned with bank-wide risk tolerance via P4 pricing mechanism
  [RAW-BOOK bcbs144 para 19–20]
```

## Measurement Framework (Principles 5–9 Core Requirements)

**Principle 5 — Cash Flow Projection:**
- Assets, liabilities, off-balance sheet items over multiple time horizons
- Four components: future cash flows, contingent liquidity demand, FX positions,
  correspondent/settlement activities [RAW-BOOK bcbs144 para 22–46]
- Early warning indicators: 17 specific signals (rapid asset growth, rating downgrade,
  widening CDS spreads, repo counterparties requesting additional collateral, etc.)
  [RAW-BOOK bcbs144 para 54]

**Principle 6 — Cross-entity Control:**
- Monitor at: individual legal entity + group consolidated level
- Account for: legal/regulatory restrictions on intragroup fund transfers
- Contagion risk: local stress can spread group-wide via reputation channel
  [RAW-BOOK bcbs144 para 58–64]

**Principle 7 — Funding Diversification:**
- Diversify by: counterparty, secured/unsecured, instrument type, currency, geography
- Limit concentration in any single source or tenor
- Maintain active market presence (not just planning documents)
- Alternative funding sources: deposit growth, MTN, securitisation, repo of HQLA,
  intragroup transfers, central bank marginal lending [RAW-BOOK bcbs144 para 74]

**Principle 9 — Collateral Management:**
- Track encumbered vs unencumbered by legal entity, jurisdiction, currency
- Assess CB eligibility for each asset class (intraday credit, standing facilities)
- Understand mobilisation timeframes including cross-border pledging
  [RAW-BOOK bcbs144 para 88–93]

## Historical Context: 2008 as Pre-LCR Standard

The 17 Principles represent the qualitative foundation that Basel III later
operationalised into quantitative ratios:

```
BCBS 2008 Principle → Basel III Quantitative Standard
  P12 (HQLA cushion)  → LCR: HQLA / 30-day net outflows ≥ 100%
  P7  (funding diversity + tenor) → NSFR: ASF / RSF ≥ 100%
  P8  (intraday)      → BCBS 2013 intraday liquidity monitoring tools
  P10 (stress tests)  → ILAAP stress scenarios; SREP liquidity adequacy
  P11 (CFP)           → Recovery Plans (BRRD); ICAAP liquidity section

The 2007–08 failures P12+P10 address:
  - Banks lacked HQLA buffers → forced fire sales → liquidity spirals
  - Stress tests ignored market-wide scenarios → contagion blindspot
  - CFPs not linked to stress test results → plans unusable in crisis
[RAW-BOOK bcbs144 para 3]
```

## Supervisory Powers (Principles 14–17 Summary)

```
P14: Comprehensive assessment (on-site + off-site) of LRM framework AND liquidity level
     → Not just process: adequacy of actual buffer, composition, stress assumptions
     → Supervisors suggest specific stress scenarios for bank's testing programme
P15: Monitor internal reports, prudential reports, market data
     → Increase frequency during stress periods
     → Standardised supervisory reporting framework for cross-bank comparisons
P16: Remedial action authority:
     → Require improved policies/controls/reporting
     → Require robust stress testing + CFP development
     → Require reduction of liquidity risk (close funding gaps, hold larger HQLA)
     → Restrict acquisitions/business expansion
     → Require higher capital (temporary bridge, not long-term solution)
     [RAW-BOOK bcbs144 para 142]
P17: Cross-border cooperation: home/host supervisors + central banks
     → Communication intensifies during firm-specific or market-wide stress
     → 8 trigger events for heightened communication listed
     [RAW-BOOK bcbs144 para 146]
```
