## Overview

The Bank for International Settlements, established 1930 in Basel, Switzerland, serves three functions: (1) a bank for central banks (deposits, reserve management, gold transactions); (2) a forum for international central bank cooperation via the Basel Process; (3) a research and standard-setting institution through its hosted committees.

## Key Committees

- **Basel Committee on Banking Supervision (BCBS):** Sets global minimum capital (Basel III, output floor), liquidity (LCR, NSFR), and leverage standards. Standards are non-binding but effectively mandatory through BCBS member jurisdictions.
- **Committee on the Global Financial System (CGFS):** Monitors systemic risks in global financial markets.
- **Financial Stability Board (FSB):** Coordinates regulatory policy across G20 (chaired by BIS-hosted secretariat). [LLM]
- **CPMI:** Sets standards for payment and settlement systems.

## Role in Basel III Endgame

The BCBS finalised the Basel III output floor (70% of standardised RWA) in December 2017, with implementation staggered through 2028. Regional implementation — US, EU, UK — diverged significantly in scope and timeline. See [[Basel_III_Endgame_Capital_Liquidity_Credit_Migration]] and [[Basel_Regional_Implementation_Dynamics]].

## Research Function

BIS Working Papers and the BIS Quarterly Review are key primary sources for empirical evidence on: central bank balance sheet effects, collateral markets, cross-border dollar liquidity, and shadow banking dynamics. The BIS Triennial Survey provides the definitive data on global FX and OTC derivatives markets.
