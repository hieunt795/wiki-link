## The Structural Shift in Sovereign Market Buyers

As central banks withdraw through quantitative tightening (QT), non-bank financial intermediaries (NBFIs) have become the marginal price-setters in sovereign bond markets. Between 2021 and 2024, NBFIs increased their share of advanced economy sovereign bond holdings by approximately 7.9 percentage points [RAW-CLIP].

This transition replaces a buyer with an unlimited balance sheet (the central bank) with a heterogeneous collection of entities — asset managers, pension funds, and hedge funds — characterized by:
- **Variable Leverage**: Some entities (hedge funds) operate with leverage ratios reaching up to 200x [RAW-CLIP].
- **Redemption Risk**: Open-ended funds are subject to sudden outflows.
- **Sensitivity to Risk Appetite**: NBFIs are more prone to rapid unwinding during global risk-off events [RAW-CLIP].

## The G30 "Perfect Storm" Warning (April 2026)

The Group of Thirty (G30) report, *Nonbank Financial Intermediation and Financial Stability: A Perfect Storm in the Making?* (co-chaired by Agustín Carstens and Klaas Knot), identifies several critical vulnerabilities:

- **The Regulatory Gap**: There is a significant lack of data quality and supervisory frameworks for tracking NBFI sovereign exposures in real-time.
- **The Liquidity Facility Gap**: In the euro area, the ECB lacks a dedicated liquidity facility for NBFIs. In contrast, the Bank of England adopted the **Contingent Non-Bank Financial Institution Repo Facility (CNRF)** in 2025, which provides repo liquidity directly to insurance corporations and pension funds under defined stress conditions [RAW-CLIP].
- **The Transformation Form Problem**: While credit risk has migrated from banks to NBFIs, much of the associated liquidity risk remains concentrated in the banking system. Prime brokerage banks are directly exposed when leveraged NBFI strategies (like the basis trade) unwind [RAW-CLIP].

## 2025 Volatility Episodes as Signals

Idiosyncratic volatility spikes in 2025 (e.g., the March 2025 Bund spike of 30bp in one day) serve as signals of this new regime. These episodes show that the market has become highly sensitive to individual triggers (fiscal news, trade policy) in the absence of a reliable marginal buyer [RAW-CLIP].

The "honest uncertainty" for policymakers is whether episodes that resolve without intervention are evidence of genuine resilience or merely "good fortune" in a regime with an incomplete institutional safety net [RAW-CLIP].

## Summary

The NBFI supervisory gap represents a structural mismatch between the systemic importance of non-bank buyers in sovereign debt markets and the maturity of the regulatory and backstop infrastructure required to manage their inherent fragilities. [LLM]
