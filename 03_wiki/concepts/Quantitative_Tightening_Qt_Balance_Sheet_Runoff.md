Quantitative Tightening is the reverse of QE: the Fed lets maturing Treasuries and MBS roll off its balance sheet rather than reinvesting proceeds.

**Transmission mechanism:**
1. Fed allows  in Treasuries to mature → does not reinvest
2. Treasury must issue new bonds to repay Fed → private investors buy those bonds
3. Investors fund purchases from bank deposits → deposits destroyed → reserves fall
4. Result: fewer bank reserves + fewer bank deposits = less system liquidity

**Critical nuance — when tightening does NOT occur:**
If investors buy QT-supply bonds using money market fund withdrawals (not bank deposits), no deposits are destroyed and tightening is minimal. During 2022-2023, significant QT occurred alongside stable MMF assets, limiting the tightening impact.

**RRP drain buffer:** Post-2022, excess cash parked in Fed RRP provided a buffer. As QT ran off reserves, the RRP declined in parallel, releasing pre-existing liquidity — preventing a reserve crunch.

**Redemption cap:** Fed sets a monthly cap (e.g. 0B/month Treasuries + 5B MBS at peak 2022-23 pace). Once the cap is hit, proceeds above the cap are reinvested.

**Atlanta Fed estimate:** .2T QT ≈ +0.29% in rates (calm) to +0.74% (volatile) over 3 years — far weaker than assumed in market commentary.
