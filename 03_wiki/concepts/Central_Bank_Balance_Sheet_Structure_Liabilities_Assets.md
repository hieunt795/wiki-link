The central bank balance sheet is the operational foundation for monetary policy.

**LIABILITIES (monetary base = how CB creates money):**
- Banknotes in circulation: primary liability; currency demand is exogenous; CB cannot refuse
- Commercial bank reserves: electronic settlement balances held at CB; policy-sensitive
  Required: minimum reserves mandated by CB
  Excess/Free: voluntary buffers above requirement (in floor systems, large and remunerated)
- Government deposits (TGA equivalent): CB is government banker; government spending/taxes drain/add reserves
- Capital and reserves: typically small; CB can operate with negative equity (unique privilege)
- Other liabilities: repo operations, FX liabilities

**ASSETS:**
- Domestic securities: outright OMO purchases (QE adds these; QT reduces them)
- Credit operations: collateralized lending to banks (repos, LTROs)
- Foreign assets: FX reserves; key for CBs with exchange rate targets
- Gold: historical; still significant for some CBs (ECB, Russia, China)

**MISCONCEPTIONS (from Rule CCBS Handbook 32):**
1. Misconception: Reserves are lent to other banks or the real economy. WRONG: Reserves stay in the CB system; only commercial bank balance sheets expand when banks lend.
2. Misconception: More reserves = more inflation directly. WRONG: Transmission depends on credit demand and bank willingness to lend; excess reserves sit idle in floor systems.

**SEIGNIORAGE:** CB earns income on interest-bearing assets funded by non-interest bearing notes. Modern risk: when policy rates exceed asset yields, CBs may record losses (ECB, Fed 2022-2024). CB losses are accountable through equity/deferred asset; do not impair operations.

**BALANCE SHEET EXPANSION TYPES:**
- Asset-driven: CB decides to purchase assets (QE); drives reserves up mechanically
- Liability-driven: government spending drives up TGA -> reserves increase passively; CB must absorb
