## Overview

The **revenue vs receipt distinction** is a foundational methodological principle in IMF Government Finance Statistics. It prevents a common classification error that distorts fiscal deficit measurement when loans to the government are mistakenly counted as revenues.

The GFS definition: **revenues** are receipts that do not give rise to an obligation of repayment. **Receipts** is the broader category — all inflows, including loan proceeds. Since loan proceeds must be repaid, they are receipts but not revenues. [RAW-BOOK Ch.3, The GFS Framework]

## How It Works

The distinction operates symmetrically on the expenditure side:

- **Expenditure** ≠ all payments. A loan repayment is a payment but not an expenditure — it discharges an obligation incurred when the loan was received. It belongs in financing, not expenditure.
- **Interest payments**, by contrast, ARE expenditure — they compensate the lender for use of funds and do not reduce the outstanding principal obligation. [RAW-BOOK Ch.3]

The practical implication: if a government borrows to cover a gap and mistakenly records loan proceeds as revenue, the fiscal deficit is understated. GFS prevents this by reserving "revenue" strictly for non-repayable inflows.

A parallel principle applies to grants: grants are unrequited (nothing is given in return) and non-repayable, so the GFS Manual classifies them with revenues as deficit-reducing items — not with financing. [RAW-BOOK Ch.3]

## Evidence and Sources

[RAW-BOOK Ch.3] Ouanes and Thakur state explicitly: "while all revenues are receipts, all receipts are not revenues. Revenues consist only of those receipts that do not give rise to an obligation of repayment. Receipts from loans to the government are not revenues, because the loans must be repaid."
