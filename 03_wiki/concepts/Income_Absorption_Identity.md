## Overview

The **income-absorption identity** is one of two fundamental accounting relationships the IMF uses to anchor macroeconomic analysis. It links the current account balance (CAB) to the difference between a country's gross national disposable income (GNDI) and its total domestic absorption (A):

  CAB = GNDI − A

This identity is *ex-post* — it holds by definition in national accounts, not as a result of market forces. A current account deficit does not require explanation from price theory; it is an accounting statement that the economy is spending more than it produces and earns.

**Absorption (A)** is total spending by domestic residents: final consumption (C) + gross investment (I) + government expenditure (G). **GNDI** is GDP adjusted for net factor income from abroad and net current transfers.

## How It Works

The identity provides the *absorption approach* to BOP analysis:

- CAB < 0 means A > GNDI: the economy absorbs more than it earns. [RAW-BOOK Ch.2]
- To reduce a deficit, policy must either raise GNDI (requires unused capacity or structural reform) or reduce A by cutting C and/or I. [RAW-BOOK Ch.2]
- The identity does NOT explain what caused the imbalance or whether it is desirable — exchange rates, interest rates, and exogenous shocks are needed for that. [RAW-BOOK Ch.2]

The absorption approach defines the policy constraint space, not the adjustment mechanism. Every claim about adjustment must still be grounded in behavioral theory.

## Evidence and Sources

[RAW-BOOK Ch.2, pp.17-19] Ouanes and Thakur derive the identity from the SNA expenditure approach: Y = C + I + G + (X − M), where (X − M) + net factor income + net transfers = CAB, and Y − C − I − G = CAB = GNDI − A. They state it is "an ex post identity that always holds" and "does not provide a theory of current account behavior."
