## Origins: From Soviet-Style Monobank to Big Four (Pre-2000)

| Era | Structure |
|-----|-----------|
| Pre-1979 | PBOC was the sole bank in China (Soviet monobank model) |
| Post-1979 | US-China integration → "Big Four" commercial banks founded (ICBC, Bank of China, CCB, Agricultural Bank) |
| 1990s | Pro-market reforms → private sector emerges; Big Four become profit-oriented |
| 1990s-2000s | Joint-stock commercial banks (JSCBs) proliferate nationwide |

**Big Four market share decline:** 80% of all deposits in 1995 → 60% by mid-2000s → continued declining. JSCBs competed aggressively for business. [RAW-CLIP Conks Shadow Shutdown]

## Why Shadow Banking Emerged (~2000)

Two structural drivers forced smaller banks to invent shadow instruments:

1. **Loan-to-deposit ratio cap (75%):** Regulators capped bank lending at 75% of deposits to prevent credit overexpansion → banks couldn't serve all demand
2. **Big Four discrimination:** State-owned Big Four favored large SOEs (state-owned enterprises) → private firms were systematically excluded from formal credit

**Result:** Smaller banks and trust companies invented a range of "shadow instruments" to channel credit outside formal bank lending. [RAW-CLIP Conks Shadow Shutdown]

## The Chinese Shadow Instruments

Unlike the US system (10+ layers of intermediation — CDOs, SIVs, CDO-cubeds), China's shadow banking was simpler but equally opaque:

| Instrument | Mechanism |
|-----------|-----------|
| **WMPs (Wealth Management Products)** | Bank deposits repackaged as "financial products" — offered above-deposit-rate yields; off-balance-sheet |
| **Entrusted loans** | Bank A lends to Bank B's customer using Bank B as conduit — avoids direct lending restrictions |
| **Trust loans** | Trust companies extend credit; banks book as "investment receivables" not loans |
| **Shadow bonds** | Off-exchange debt instruments issued by local government entities or corporates |

**Shadow banking arms:** Mainly trust companies + securities firms operated by banks. These entities weren't subject to the 75% loan-deposit cap → could extend credit freely. [RAW-CLIP Conks Shadow Shutdown]

## Peak and Decline

| Period | Shadow Banking Share of GDP | Event |
|--------|-----------------------------|-------|
| 2016 | ~90% of nominal GDP | Peak; P2P lending bubble collapses |
| 2016+ | Regulatory crackdown begins | CBIRC imposes penalties on WMPs, trust products |
| Q2 2023 | ~39% of GDP | Continued regulatory suppression |

**Trigger for shutdown:** In 2016, collapse of a shadow peer-to-peer lending bubble pushed regulators past tolerance. The CBIRC (China Banking and Insurance Regulatory Commission) imposed cascading penalties on the most popular shadow instruments.

**2025 trajectory:** Shadow assets forecast to decline further under Basel III integration + CBIRC tiering system for risk monitoring. But as global growth slows and formal credit tightens, regulatory arbitrage incentives may revive shadow banking activity ("cat and mouse" cycle). [RAW-CLIP Conks Shadow Shutdown]

## The Basel Gamification Parallel

The US subprime CDO boom had an analog mechanism to China's WMPs:

**US (Basel I, 1988):** Basel I imposed minimum capital requirements → banks invented CDOs/CDS to "transfer credit risk" → required less capital for same returns. CDOs weren't genuine risk reduction — just redistribution among interconnected shadow actors. CDO-cubeds = riskiest protection repackaged multiple times to achieve highest rating.

**China (~2000):** Loan-to-deposit cap (equivalent of a balance sheet constraint) → banks repackaged deposits as WMPs / booked loans as "investment receivables" → maintained the appearance of regulatory compliance while expanding credit off-balance-sheet.

**Core dynamic:** Both systems exploited a gap between regulatory categorization and economic substance. Regulation → regulatory arbitrage → shadow instruments → systemic risk accumulation. [LLM synthesis from RAW-CLIP Conks Shadow Shutdown]

## Post-GFC US Shadow System vs. Chinese System

| Dimension | US Post-GFC | China Peak (2016) |
|-----------|------------|-------------------|
| Primary instruments | Repos, ABS, CLOs | WMPs, trust loans, entrusted loans |
| Intermediation layers | Multi-layer (MMFs → dealers → hedge funds) | Fewer layers (bank → trust company → borrower) |
| Collateral type | Securitized loans, agency MBS, Treasuries | "Investment receivables," bank deposits |
| Regulatory response | Basel III, Dodd-Frank | CBIRC crackdown, Basel III integration |
| Status (2024) | Stable, regulated | Declining, residual shadow activity remains |
