thesis: '[LLM] Central banks use FX options primarily as buyers (purchasing the right
  to intervene at a strike price) rather than sellers, because selling options creates
  unlimited downside exposure. Cancelable forwards give the CB the right to cancel
  delivery if market conditions change, functioning as a forward plus an embedded
  option — useful for signaling commitment while preserving optionality, but at a
  wider bid-ask spread. Zero-cost structures (seagull, range forward) create rate
  corridors with no upfront premium but bind the CB to deliver within a range, converting
  discretionary intervention into a semi-automatic rule.'
