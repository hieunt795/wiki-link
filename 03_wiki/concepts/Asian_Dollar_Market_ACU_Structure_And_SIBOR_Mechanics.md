## Overview
The **Asian Dollar Market** was the Asian branch of the global eurodollar system, centered in Singapore through ACU (Asian Currency Unit) banks. [LLM] It operated in parallel with London's eurodollar market but served Asian time-zone borrowers and exploited Singapore's distinct regulatory advantages.

## Mechanics

**Liability side (funding):**
- ACU banks issued dollar-denominated CDs, time deposits, and bonds to offshore dollar holders (MNCs, foreign banks, overseas Chinese wealthy investors, foreign central banks)
- These instruments were SIBOR-linked (Singapore Interbank Offered Rate)
- Buyers credited ACU banks' US correspondent accounts with Fed reserves to settle

**Asset side (lending):**
- ACU banks lent accumulated dollar claims to regional borrowers at SIBOR+spread
- A thriving interbank market formed as banks traded Asian dollar liabilities among themselves via an order-book system

**Settlement backbone:**
- All dollar transfers ultimately cleared through US correspondent accounts at the Fed — identical to the London eurodollar settlement mechanism [RAW-CLIP]

## Regulatory Arbitrage
Singapore offered more profitable arbitrage than domestic US dollar markets:
- Lower reserve requirements on offshore dollar books
- Lighter regulatory oversight than US onshore banking
- This attracted global banks (JPMorgan, Bank of America HQ offices) as dollar suppliers

## Post-GFC Decline
The 2008 GFC triggered global regulatory pressure on unsecured lending, which was the lifeblood of the Asian dollar market. [LLM] Post-reform, ACU bank activity contracted sharply as Basel III LCR made unsecured interbank borrowing prohibitively expensive.

## Regional FX Market Share (Post-Decline)
Despite the unsecured market's contraction, the major centers retained FX intermediation roles:
- Singapore: ~20% of global FX turnover
- Hong Kong: ~7%
- Tokyo: ~4.5%

These centers now intermediate FX primarily via secured/derivatives channels rather than the historic unsecured deposit market. [RAW-CLIP]
