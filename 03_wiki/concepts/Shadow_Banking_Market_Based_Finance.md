Shadow banking encompasses credit intermediation outside traditional deposit-taking banks.

**Origin — Basel I arbitrage (late 1980s):**
Basel I imposed minimum capital requirements on bank loans. Banks responded by securitizing loans into CDOs and ABS, requiring less capital for the same economic exposure. GSEs (Fannie, Freddie, FHLB) pioneered the originate-to-distribute model.

**Pre-2008 structure:** Multi-layer intermediation chain — ABCP conduits → SIVs → CDOs → CDO² → CDO³. Risk was dispersed but remained interconnected; stress at any layer cascaded.

**Post-2008 structure:** Collapsed to structurally essential components:
- Repos (primary funding mechanism for securities dealers)
- Money market funds (short-term cash management)
- ABS/CLOs (securitized credit to real economy)
Basel II/III contained extreme leverage but did not eliminate the system.

**Chinese parallel:** China developed its own shadow system via wealth management products (WMPs), trust loans, entrusted loans — reached 90% of GDP by 2016. Regulatory crackdown reduced it to ~39% by Q2 2023.

**Systemic role:** Shadow banks are now essential to dollar funding markets — repos alone clear T+ daily. Stress in shadow banking (ABCP freeze 2007, repo seizure 2008, Sept 2019 repo spike) transmits rapidly to the real economy.
