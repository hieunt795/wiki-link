## Regime Definition
[LLM] Stagflation is not just "high inflation plus weak growth"; it is a macro regime where the inflation impulse does not come from excess demand, while output and employment are already under pressure.

[RAW-BOOK §Inflation] The IMF text distinguishes demand-pull inflation from cost-push inflation and says cost-push inflation can develop even when unemployment is high and resource utilization is low.

[LLM] That distinction is the core diagnostic marker: demand-led overheating and stagflation can look similar in headline CPI, but they require different policy interpretation.

## Historical Template
[RAW-BOOK §Inflation] The IMF text identifies oil price increases in the mid-1970s and early 1980s as examples where an initial cost-push phenomenon turned into persistent inflation in a number of oil-importing countries.

[RAW-BOOK §Inflation] The same passage contrasts Japan, which experienced a large energy shock but kept inflation under control because policy was non-accommodating and the population accepted a temporary real-income reduction.

[LLM] The historical lesson is that the same external energy shock can produce different inflation outcomes depending on monetary accommodation, wage behavior, credibility, and real-income absorption.

## Diagnostic Grid
| Diagnostic axis | Stagflation signal | Non-stagflation signal |
|---|---|---|
| Inflation impulse | [RAW-BOOK §Inflation] Energy, import, wage, or productivity cost pressure | [RAW-BOOK §Inflation] Excess aggregate demand |
| Real activity | [RAW-BOOK §Employment] Falling output or weak labor demand | [LLM] Output above potential |
| Wage behavior | [RAW-BOOK §Inflation] Wages rising faster than productivity | [LLM] Wages aligned with productivity |
| Policy risk | [LLM] Tightening worsens activity; easing validates inflation | [LLM] Tightening mainly cools demand |

## Contemporary Extension
[RAW-CLIP] The 2026 central-bank commentary says modern supply shocks may propagate through production-network cascades before expectations or wages move.

[LLM] This adds a current diagnostic layer: a stagflation risk can emerge from input-output bottlenecks even before the classic wage-price spiral is visible.

[LLM] The framework therefore treats stagflation as a two-stage problem: first identify whether the shock is cost-side, then determine whether it is becoming persistent through wages, expectations, contracts, or production-network amplification.
