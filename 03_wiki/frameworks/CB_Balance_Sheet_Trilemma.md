## Overview
Proposed by Fed researchers (Duygan-Bump & Kahn) and debated by Roc Armenter in 2026, the **Balance Sheet Trilemma** explains the structural constraints facing modern central banks in a post-QE world [RAW-CLIP].

## The Three Vertices
1.  **Small Balance Sheet:** Returning to a "lean" pre-2008 size.
2.  **Low Short-Term Rate Volatility:** Keeping market rates (SOFR, FFR) tightly anchored to policy targets.
3.  **Limited Market Intervention:** Avoiding frequent "Desk" operations or standing facilities that replace private interbank activity.

## Strategic Choices
- **The "Ample Reserves" Choice (1+2):** Sacrifice the small balance sheet (vertex 1) to ensure rate stability (vertex 2) without constant intervention (vertex 3). This is the current Fed regime [RAW-CLIP].
- **The "Scarce Reserves" Choice (2+3):** Sacrifice the small balance sheet (vertex 1) and use frequent interventions (vertex 3) to keep rates stable (vertex 2).
- **The "Warsh Shift" Choice (Attempting to break the triangle):** Kevin Warsh and critics of the trilemma argue that by changing "global variables" (e.g., reducing TGA volatility, relaxing liquidity regulations), the central bank can shift the frontier and achieve a smaller balance sheet without excessive volatility [RAW-CLIP].

## Breaking the Trilemma (2026 Proposals)
- **TGA Management:** Reducing the level and volatility of the Treasury General Account could reduce balance sheet needs by up to **2 percentage points of Nominal GDP** [RAW-CLIP].
- **Regulatory Diet:** Reducing "liquidity dependence" by enabling banks to better redistribute reserves (interbank market revival) could lower the structural demand for reserves [RAW-CLIP].
- **Standing Facilities:** Using facilities like the **SRF** as an "outside option" to control rates without actual lending, thereby tempering volatility even with a smaller balance sheet [RAW-CLIP].

## Case Study: Japan 2026
Japan is currently failing the trilemma: it has a large balance sheet and is attempting to limit rate volatility, but it is forced into **heavy-handed intervention** (FX intervention and YCC) because its "global variables" (debt-to-GDP, demographics) are unhedged and extremely sensitive to rate moves [LLM].
