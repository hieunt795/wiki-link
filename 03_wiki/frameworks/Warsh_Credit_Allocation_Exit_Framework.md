## Core Claim
[RAW-CLIP] The Accord source says the Fed needs to get out of credit allocation.

[RAW-CLIP] It specifically uses mortgage-backed securities as the example of taking from Peter to pay Paul.

[LLM] The doctrine is that once the Fed starts selecting sectors or assets to support, it crosses from monetary implementation into political distribution.

## Why Warsh Wants Exit
[LLM] Warsh's logic is that a central bank should not be the hidden subsidy allocator in a market economy.

[LLM] If housing or another sector needs support, the choice should be explicit, budgeted, and made by the Treasury or Congress rather than being embedded in the asset side of the Fed balance sheet.

[LLM] That makes the subsidy visible, taxable, and politically answerable.

## Operating Consequences
[LLM] An exit from credit allocation would imply fewer permanent holdings of sector-specific assets, fewer distortions in the term structure, and less confusion about whether the Fed is doing monetary policy or industrial policy.

[LLM] It also pushes the Fed toward a narrower toolkit: reserve provision, short-term rate control, and temporary market-function support only when explicitly justified.

## Boundary Condition
[LLM] The doctrine does not deny that crises happen.

[LLM] It argues that crisis support should not become a standing quasi-fiscal program, because that erodes both transparency and independence.
