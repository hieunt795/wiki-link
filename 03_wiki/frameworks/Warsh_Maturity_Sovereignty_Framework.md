## Core Claim
[RAW-CLIP] The Accord source says the Fed and Treasury need to agree on who is in charge of the maturity structure of government debt.

[RAW-CLIP] It also says that if the Fed buys long-term debt, it should either buy only short-term bonds or swap the interest-rate risk back to the Treasury.

[LLM] The practical doctrine is that debt-duration management belongs on the fiscal side, because that is where the government's funding strategy and risk budget belong.

## Why Warsh Cares About Maturity
[RAW-CLIP] The clipping argues that QE undoes some of the Treasury's interest-rate protection by swapping long-term debt into overnight reserves.

[LLM] That is the key maturity-sovereignty objection: the Treasury deliberately issued long debt to lock in funding costs, while the Fed later re-exposed the public sector to short-rate risk.

[LLM] From Warsh's perspective, that is not just a balance-sheet detail. It is a form of quasi-fiscal intervention.

## Operating Implications
[LLM] Under this framework, the Fed should not be the default institution deciding how much duration the sovereign system carries.

[LLM] If market function requires temporary intervention, the intervention should be narrow, explicit, and paired with a prompt transfer of maturity risk back to the Treasury.

[LLM] That preserves the Fed as a monetary operator while leaving fiscal duration choice to the elected fiscal authority.

## Constraint
[LLM] The framework is attractive politically because it simplifies the Fed's role.

[LLM] The constraint is practical: the Treasury must be willing and able to manage duration more actively, otherwise the market may demand the Fed to step in during stress.

[LLM] So maturity sovereignty is a doctrine of responsibility transfer, not an elimination of crisis support.
