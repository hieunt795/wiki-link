Bindseil defines three core instruments of monetary policy implementation:

**1. Open Market Operations (OMOs):** CB-initiated transactions with banks.
- Outright: purchase/sale of securities (changes CB balance sheet permanently)
- Reverse/credit operations: collateralized lending via auction (repo/tender)

**2. Standing Facilities:** Bank-initiated at any time within CB commitment.
- Borrowing facility (marginal lending/Lombard): banks borrow at penalty rate -> ceiling of corridor
- Deposit facility: banks deposit excess at remunerated rate -> floor of corridor
- Together they bracket overnight rates within the corridor

**3. Reserve Requirements:** Minimum sight deposits banks must maintain at CB.
- Averaging provision: requirement measured over maintenance period (e.g. 1 month)
- Key functions: buffer for autonomous factor volatility; tool for liquidity management
- Post-2008 trend: many CBs reduced/eliminated reserve requirements as floor systems made them redundant

**Implementation vs. Macroeconomics (Separation Principle):**
Monetary macroeconomics identifies the optimal short-term rate level (transmission analysis).
Implementation ensures that level prevails via market operations. The two functions are segregated within CBs (economics dept vs. market operations dept). This dichotomy breaks down in crises when the overnight rate loses adequacy as the sole operational target.

**Operational target:** The short-term interest rate that (i) can be controlled by the CB, (ii) influences the ultimate target, (iii) defines monetary stance, (iv) guides implementation officers.
