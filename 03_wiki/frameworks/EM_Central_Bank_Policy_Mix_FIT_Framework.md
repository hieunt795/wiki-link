## The Pre-GFC Paradigm: Single-Instrument ITF

Pre-GFC consensus: central banks focus on **Inflation Targeting Framework (ITF)** — one instrument (short-term interest rate) for one goal (price stability). The two-decade "Great Moderation" appeared to validate this approach: low inflation, strong growth.

**GFC lesson**: Price stability alone is insufficient — macrofinancial imbalances can build during periods of price stability and low rates (credit booms, asset bubbles, excessive leverage). "There is no macrostability without financial stability." [RAW-CLIP]

## The New Paradigm: Four-Pillar Policy Mix

| Pillar | Tool | Target |
|--------|------|--------|
| 1. Flexible IT (FIT) | Policy rate + FX intervention | Price stability + exchange rate stability |
| 2. Macroprudential | Capital buffers, LTV, LDR, CCyB | Financial system stability, procyclicality dampening |
| 3. Capital Flow Management | Administrative + market-based CFM | Exchange rate stability, sudden-stop prevention |
| 4. Payment System | Regulation, infrastructure | Systemic risk in payment networks |

[RAW-CLIP]

## Why "Flexible" Inflation Targeting (FIT) in EMEs

Standard ITF: strictly focus on inflation; let exchange rate float freely.

**FIT for EMEs**: Acknowledge that in small open economies, exchange rate volatility directly transmits into inflation and financial stability. The CB must manage:
1. Interest rate (primary monetary tool)
2. FX intervention (secondary stabilization)
3. Capital flow management (to navigate impossible trinity) [RAW-CLIP]

**The Impossible Trinity**: Free capital flows + monetary independence + fixed exchange rate cannot all coexist. EMEs face: large, volatile portfolio flows that overwhelm domestic financial markets if unchecked. FIT + CFM resolves this by treating CFM as a third policy instrument. [RAW-CLIP]

## Capital Flow Management (CFM) Tools

**Administrative/quantitative**:
- Capital controls on portfolio inflows (e.g. Tobin tax variants)
- Minimum holding periods for non-resident bond investors
- Restrictions on short-term external borrowing

**Market-based**:
- FX intervention (buying/selling FX reserves)
- Interest rate adjustment to manage carry trade incentives [RAW-CLIP]

**Key principle**: CFM is not capital controls to restrict globalization benefits, but a policy tool to smooth excessive short-term volatility while preserving medium-term capital flow openness. [RAW-CLIP]

## Policy Mix Coordination Principle (Cecchetti & Kohler 2012)

When monetary policy (interest rate) and macroprudential policy (capital requirements/CCyB) are:
- **Independent**: Each overshoots when the other doesn't react → public welfare losses larger
- **Coordinated**: Optimal solution — interest rate doesn't overtighten if macroprudential can handle credit excess; CCyB doesn't need to fully tighten if rate already adjusting

Conclusion: tight coordination between monetary and macroprudential policy produces the lowest social welfare loss for both demand and supply shocks. [RAW-CLIP]

## BI (Bank Indonesia) as Case Study

BI implemented the policy mix concept in 2010, becoming one of the first central banks to formally combine:
- BI 7-day Repo Rate (monetary policy)
- LTV ratio, down payment requirements (macroprudential)
- Capital flow measures (FX intervention + FX reserve requirements)
- Payment system oversight

The BI Rate was renamed BI 7-Day Repo Rate in 2016 to better align with its actual monetary stance (from 12-month rate to 7-day rate). [RAW-CLIP]

The BI experience validated the policy mix framework as operationally feasible for EMEs navigating the impossible trinity. [LLM]
