## Core Claim
[RAW-CLIP] The source explicitly says the Treasury-Fed cash arrangement is "a choice" and that it has operated differently in the past.

[LLM] This framework reads that statement literally: the current placement of Treasury cash at the Fed is an operational convention, not an unavoidable feature of the monetary system.

[LLM] The arrangement can therefore be analyzed as institutional design space with multiple feasible configurations, each carrying different consequences for reserves, balance-sheet size, and operational complexity.

## What Is Structural
[RAW-CLIP] The Treasury still needs a fiscal agent, and the Fed still serves that role in the present setup.

[LLM] Some things are structural: the government needs settlement rails, cash accounts, and a reliable way to move funds between public and private balance sheets.

[LLM] Those functions do not disappear if the arrangement changes.

## What Is Redesignable
[LLM] What can change is where Treasury cash is held, how much idle balance the Treasury maintains, and how much volatility the Fed must absorb in its own liabilities.

[LLM] That matters because the TGA is not merely a storage account; it is a liability that sits directly on the Fed balance sheet and interacts with reserves.

[LLM] If the operating convention were redesigned, the Fed might need fewer reserves to keep overnight rates stable, or it might need a different menu of reserve-management operations.

## Policy Significance
[LLM] The practical significance of the framework is that balance-sheet growth linked to the TGA is partly endogenous to institutional design.

[LLM] That means discussions about Fed balance-sheet reduction cannot stop at QE or QT alone.

[LLM] They must also consider whether Treasury cash management rules, account-placement rules, and the Fed-Treasury interface itself should change.

[LLM] In short, the mechanism is not just accounting; it is governance.
