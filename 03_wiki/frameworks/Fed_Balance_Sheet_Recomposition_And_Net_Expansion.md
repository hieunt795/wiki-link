## Size, Mix, and Duration
[LLM] Three different things are often conflated in Fed balance-sheet debates: total size, asset composition, and duration profile.

[LLM] Total size is the balance-sheet quantity headline.
[LLM] Composition is the split between bills, notes, bonds, and MBS.
[LLM] Duration is the portfolio's interest-rate sensitivity.

## What the Sources Say
[RAW-CLIP] The Accord source says the Treasury should be in charge of the maturity structure of government debt, and that if the Fed buys long-term debt it should either buy only short-term bonds or swap the interest-rate risk back to the Treasury.

[RAW-CLIP] The same source says the Fed should get out of credit allocation, because buying MBS is a political decision rather than a neutral monetary one.

[RAW-CLIP] The Warsh clipping rejects the idea that shrinking the balance sheet necessarily creates "room" for rate cuts when the balance sheet was only meeting structural demand for reserves.

## Balance-Sheet Arithmetic
[LLM] A short-UST purchase paired with MBS or long-bond runoff can make the Fed's holdings more bill-heavy and less duration-heavy without implying a new wave of QE.

[LLM] The balance sheet only expands if purchases are net of runoff and sales.

| Action | Total assets | Duration profile | Sector allocation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Buy short USTs | + if net positive | shorter | more Treasury-heavy |
| Let MBS run off | - if not replaced | shorter | less mortgage exposure |
| Buy short USTs while MBS/long bonds run off | ambiguous | shorter | cleaner and more bill-heavy |

[LLM] So the policy distinction is not "smaller vs larger" alone.
[LLM] It is "what is being bought, what is being allowed to run off, and whether net purchases exceed the runoff path."

## Policy Implication
[LLM] If the Fed wants reserves for plumbing reasons, short-term bill purchases fit the reserve-management logic better than renewed long-duration QE.

[LLM] If the Fed wants to reduce mortgage exposure or undo credit allocation, MBS runoff or sales move the balance sheet in that direction without requiring a claim that every composition change is stimulative.

[LLM] The cleanest reading is that the Fed can make the balance sheet shorter and more Treasury-heavy while keeping total assets flat, rising, or falling depending on the net flow.
