The global dollar system encompasses all offshore USD funding markets interconnected through institutional dependencies.

**26+ markets (BIS taxonomy):**
FX swaps, cross-currency basis swaps, Eurodollar deposits, international repo, offshore USD bond issuance, dollar-denominated trade finance, correspondent banking.

**Structural interdependencies:**
- French banks: major lenders in European FX swap market and repo intermediaries for Japan and Canada
- US bank European branches: delever quarterly, send Eurodollars into Fed RRP
- Australian banks: provide FX swaps to European counterparts
Each node failure cascades system-wide — exit is near-impossible without severe financial pain.

**Why dollar hegemony persists:**
1. US Treasury market is the only asset with sufficient depth for global collateral (8T+ outstanding)
2. Repo (T+ daily) requires safe, liquid collateral — only Treasuries qualify at scale
3. Network effects: every institution has dollar-denominated obligations and assets
4. No alternative currency has deep enough capital markets or creditor-of-last-resort capacity

**Fed role in offshore dollar:** Fed swap lines with major CBs (ECB, BOJ, BOE, SNB, BOC) act as offshore lender of last resort. Without swap lines, dollar shortage in March 2020 would have been catastrophic.

**Treasury market as anchor:** Since most global loans are secured against Treasury paper, Treasury market illiquidity (MOVE index surging) destabilizes the entire global dollar architecture.
