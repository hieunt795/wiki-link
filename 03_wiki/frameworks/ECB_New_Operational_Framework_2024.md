## Overview
In March 2024, the ECB Governing Council announced a new operational framework for implementing monetary policy, which began implementation in September 2024. This framework represents a transition from the "floor system" characterized by massive excess reserves (from QE) toward a hybrid system designed to remain effective even as the balance sheet shrinks [RAW-CLIP].

## Core Components

### 1. The DFR as the Primary Policy Rate
The **Deposit Facility Rate (DFR)** remains the primary instrument for steering the monetary policy stance. Short-term money market rates (specifically the **€STR**) are expected to evolve in the vicinity of the DFR [RAW-CLIP]. 

### 2. Narrowed MRO-DFR Spread
The spread between the **Main Refinancing Operation (MRO)** rate and the DFR was narrowed to **15 basis points** (down from 50 bps). This narrow spread acts as a tool to limit volatility above the DFR [RAW-CLIP].
- **MRO:** Conducted via **Fixed Rate Full Allotment (FRFA)**, allowing banks to demand as much liquidity as needed weekly [RAW-CLIP].
- **MLF (Marginal Lending Facility):** Remains the ceiling of the corridor, providing emergency liquidity.

### 3. Structural Reserves Layer
The framework emphasizes the need for a "structural" layer of reserves to ensure the market rate stays near the DFR. This is achieved through:
- **Structural Portfolio:** Securities purchases (to be introduced later) to cover the banking sector’s structural liquidity needs [RAW-CLIP].
- **Structural LTROs:** Longer-term refinancing operations with longer maturities [RAW-CLIP].

## Mechanism: Range Floor System
The system is described as a "soft floor" or "range floor" because the overnight unsecured rate is maintained within a narrow range (15 bps) above the DFR [RAW-CLIP]. 
- **Convergence Logic:** Banks with excess reserves will not lend below the DFR (the "hard floor" for they can always deposit at the CB), while borrowing banks can access unlimited liquidity via MRO at DFR + 15 bps (the "soft ceiling") [LLM].
- **Difference from QE:** Unlike QE, which aimed to influence long-term rates via massive balance sheet expansion, the structural portfolio in the new framework is calibrated solely to meet bank demand for reserves and autonomous factors (like banknotes in circulation) [RAW-CLIP].

## Comparison with Pre-2008 Framework
| Feature | Pre-2008 (Corridor) | New 2024 (Range Floor) |
|---------|---------------------|-------------------------|
| **Anchor Rate** | MRO | DFR |
| **Liquidity Supply** | Controlled Auction (Scarce) | Full Allotment (Ample/Demand-driven) |
| **Spread** | Wide (e.g., 100-200 bps) | Narrow (15 bps) |
| **Balance Sheet** | Small/Lean | Larger (Structural reserves) |
