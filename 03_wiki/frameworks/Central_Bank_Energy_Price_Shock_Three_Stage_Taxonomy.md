thesis: '[LLM] Central banks apply a three-part taxonomy when responding to energy
  price shocks: (1) direct effects — immediate pass-through to headline CPI via energy
  prices; (2) indirect effects — cost pass-through via energy-intensive intermediate
  goods and production inputs; (3) second-round effects — changes in wage- and price-setting
  behaviour that outlast the original shock. Policy is calibrated to look through
  direct and indirect effects unless second-round effects materialize (expectations
  drift), but this framework may miss structural cascade propagation that precedes
  any expectations move.'
