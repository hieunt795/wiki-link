## Overview

Basel standards are set internationally by the BCBS but implemented nationally, creating regulatory arbitrage and divergent market structures. The key differentiator is not the regulation itself but the *starting structure of the banking system* — how bank-centric the economy is before Basel hits. [LLM]

## Regional Profiles

### United States — Mature Market, Gold-Plating
| Dimension | Detail |
|-----------|--------|
| Bank reliance for corporate debt | ~20–25% (bank-centric) vs. 75–80% capital markets |
| Basel III scope expansion | G-SIB + all banks >$100B assets |
| Expected capital increase | G-SIBs +21%, regional banks +10% |
| Implementation timeline | July 2025 start, 3-year transition to July 2028 |
| PC market size | ~$1.34T AUM (Nth America); ~$2.1T global |
| Primary vehicle | BDCs (Business Development Companies) — publicly listed, retail-accessible |

Key dynamic: US banks shifted to originate-to-distribute earliest; the CLO market and BDC structure were already developed. Basel III Endgame is an *acceleration* of an existing trend.

### European Union — Bank-Centric, Maximum Output Floor Shock
| Dimension | Detail |
|-----------|--------|
| Bank reliance for corporate debt | ~85% traditional (vs. 15–25% capital markets) |
| Output Floor impact | Largest single Basel III effect: +21.3% MRC, 41.4% of total Basel III impact on Group 1 banks |
| Share of Group 1 banks affected | ~80% constrained by Output Floor + Leverage Ratio |
| PC access vehicle | ELTIF 2.0 (cross-border retail access, modeled on BDC concept) |
| PC market status | Rapidly growing "white space" — most structural growth opportunity globally |

Key dynamic: European banks historically used advanced IRB models to minimize specialized lending capital. Output Floor eliminates this advantage for ~80% of large banks simultaneously, forcing the largest structural retreat from corporate credit.

### APAC & Vietnam — Flexible Implementation, Delayed Catalyst
| Dimension | Detail |
|-----------|--------|
| Basel application | Flexible; state influence limits pure capital-discipline implementation |
| PC market (2024) | ~$59B, projected +46% to ~$92B by 2027 |
| Vietnam: Key regulation | Thông tư 14/2025 (Basel III): CAR path 8.625% → 10.5% by 2030 |
| Vietnam: Credit quotas | Removal planned 2026 → shift to capital-based discipline |
| Vietnam: TPDN crisis | ~230 trillion VND bonds matured 2023–2024; Decree 08/2023 extended up to 2 years |
| Vietnam: PC entry mode | Special situations: M&A, distressed real estate, minority buyouts; often via preference shares due to FX control rules |

Key dynamic: Vietnam is 5–8 years behind the US in Basel-driven PC development, but the combination of credit quota removal + CAR tightening + TPDN market freeze creates conditions for rapid PC emergence in specialized segments.
