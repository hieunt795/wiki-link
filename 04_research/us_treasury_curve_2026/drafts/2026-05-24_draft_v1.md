## TOP-DOWN ENTRY

**Regime Status (CURRENT 🟢):** The US Treasury curve is positively sloped for the first time in ~18 months. 2y=4.13%, 10y=4.56%, 30y=5.07% as of May 22, 2026 [WEB-2026-05-22]. The steepening is driven from the long end (bear steepener), not the short end — fiscal supply, term premium normalization, and foreign demand retreat are setting the pace, not imminent Fed cuts.

**Inversion was LEGACY 🟡:** The 2022–2024 inversion (peak -110bps in 2s10s) reflected aggressive Fed hiking into a market pricing eventual easing. That regime is closed.

**Old QE suppression of term premium is DEPRECATED 🔴:** Term premium was near-zero or negative 2016–2022 because the Fed was buying duration. ACM 10y at 0.68% [WEB-2026-04-24] is the new floor, not the ceiling, under current fiscal/supply dynamics.

### 1.2 Curve Regime: Bear Steepener

**Definition:** A bear steepener is a curve configuration where long yields rise more than short yields (or short yields fall), increasing the slope. It indicates that duration risk and/or fiscal/inflation uncertainty are driving the long end up independently of the Fed's near-term rate path.

Current drivers confirm bear steepener classification:
- 2y anchored by FOMC hold at 3.50–3.75% + ~1 cut priced for 2026 → 4.13% [LLM-E based on rate structure]
- 10y and 30y rising on fiscal supply, term premium normalization, and Japan selling [WEB-2026-05-24]
- Curve did NOT steepen from bull side: the Fed did not cause a large rally in short rates to produce the slope

### 1.3 Historical Context

The 2022–2024 inversion ran for approximately 25 months — one of the longest on record. The unwinding produced a classic pattern: short end falls as Fed begins cutting (Sep 2024 first cut), long end initially rallies then reverses as fiscal/supply premium overrides rate relief. The result is a curve that is technically positive but with only 43bps of slope — historically thin (pre-GFC normal was 100-200bps in 2s10s).

IF the FOMC cuts 3-5 times in 2026-2027 AND the long end stays anchored at current levels, THEN 2s10s would steepen to 100-150bps — a full normalization [LLM-E].
IF the FOMC holds all year AND the long end rises another 30-50bps on fiscal concerns, THEN 2s10s could widen to 70-80bps from the supply side alone [LLM-E].

### 2.2 Term Premium: Regime Shift, Not Spike

ACM 10y term premium at 0.68% (April 2026) represents a structural regime shift, not a transitory spike:

**Pre-2022 regime (suppressed):** QE purchases absorbed ~$3.5T of duration from private sector between 2008 and 2022. Academic estimates (Gagnon, Krishnamurthy-Vissing-Jorgensen) attributed ~100-150bps of term premium suppression to QE peak holdings. Term premium was zero or negative because the Fed was the marginal buyer of duration.

**Post-QT regime (normalizing):** QT2 removed $2.2T from SOMA. The Fed now buys only T-bills (RMPs). Duration supply must clear through price-sensitive private buyers → term premium must be positive to attract them. 0.68% is consistent with the partial unwinding of QE suppression.

**The upper bound question:** Ferrante Capital (2026) estimates the taper tantrum of 2013 moved term premium by ~100bps in 4 months [WEB-2026-05-24]. With current supply dynamics, a term premium of 1.0-1.5% is plausible if: (a) fiscal path deteriorates, (b) Japan selling accelerates, or (c) Fed signals no near-term cuts.

IF term premium rises from 0.68% to 1.50% (+82bps), THEN 10y yield rises to ~5.38% with no change in rate expectations [LLM-E]. This would represent a bear steepening shock scenario.

### 2.3 Real Rate: r* or Supply Clearing?

The implied real 10y yield of ~2.08% is elevated relative to history:
- 2010-2019 average: ~0.5-1.5%
- Current FOMC r* estimate (Holston-Laubach-Williams, 2025 update): ~1.0-1.5% [LLM-E]
- TIPS 10y yield reflects: r* + real term premium + any liquidity/supply premium

IF the true r* is ~1.25% and real term premium is ~0.50%, THEN the remaining ~0.33% is supply-clearing premium above fundamentals [LLM-E].
IF supply concerns resolve (e.g., debt ceiling deal reduces near-term issuance), THEN real yields compress and nominal 10y rallies by ~25-35bps without any change in inflation expectations or Fed path [LLM-E].

**The breakeven structure's message:** The 5y5y at 2.29% implies that even 5-10 years from now, the market expects inflation to sit 29bps above target. This is consistent with one of two scenarios:

**Scenario A (tariff pass-through, base case):** Tariffs cause a one-time price level shift (3-6 months of elevated CPI/PCE) but not a durable change in inflation dynamics. The 29bps above target is an "inflation uncertainty premium" priced by investors who are unsure how long the tariff effects last. IF inflation falls toward 2.0% by late 2027 per the FOMC staff baseline, THEN 5y5y anchors back to 2.0-2.1% and term premium from inflation uncertainty compresses. [LLM-E]

**Scenario B (structurally embedded, tail risk):** Tariff regime becomes permanent; companies price product cycles with new input cost structure. IF tariffs remain in place for 3+ years AND second-round wage effects emerge, THEN 5y5y drifts toward 2.5-3.0% → term premium does not compress → long end stays elevated or rises. [LLM-E]

**Current market is pricing 90%+ of Scenario A** — 2.29% is close enough to 2% that it reflects uncertainty premium, not a structural break. The signal to watch is whether 5y5y diverges above 2.5% on a sustained basis.

## LENS 3 — MECHANISM: Four Structural Drivers of Long-End Elevation

### 3.1 Driver 1 — Fiscal Supply Pressure

**T-account chain:**

Treasury deficit → coupon issuance ↑ → auction supply ↑ → dealers must bid ↑ → dealer warehousing capacity strained [RAW-WIKI Treasury_Market_Dealer_Intermediation_Capacity] → clearing yield must rise ↑ → long-end yield elevated

**Quantification:**
- FY2026 deficit: **$1.9T** [WEB-2026-05-24]
- May 2026 refunding: $125B coupons (3y $58B + 10y $42B + 30y $25B) [WEB-2026-05-24]
- Forward implication: $1.3T projected FY2027-28 shortfall at current sizes → market prices in future coupon increases today

**The forward supply premium mechanism [LLM-E]:**
Markets are forward-looking. IF investors believe coupon auction sizes will increase by FY2027-28 (to fill the $1.3T shortfall), THEN buying a 30y bond today means holding duration through that future supply surge → premium required now to compensate for that future risk → term premium embedded even before the supply materializes.

**SLR/LCR amplifier [RAW-WIKI Slr_Lcr_Balance_Sheet_Constraints_Treasury_Market_Dealer]:**
Primary dealers cannot scale intermediation linearly with supply because SLR treats Treasuries as consuming capital. The capacity gap (Duffie 2026: treasury outstanding ~$28T vs. dealer capacity growing slower) means each marginal auction clears at a higher yield premium than the prior one.

### 3.2 Driver 2 — Fed Duration Withdrawal (Reverse Twist)

**T-account chain:**

QT2 ends Dec 2025 → Fed buys T-bills only (RMPs) → MBS portfolio shrinks via prepayments → prepayment proceeds → T-bills (not coupons) → SOMA duration ↓ → private sector must absorb incremental duration with no Fed backstop → clearing yield ↑

**Quantification:**
- SOMA MBS: $1.98T, declining [from fed_framework_2026 research]
- At ~$25-30B/month prepayment speed, SOMA loses ~$300B of MBS duration per year → all replaced with T-bills → net ~$300B annual duration release to private market [LLM-E]

**Key distinction from QE reversal:**
QE actively suppressed term premium by buying duration at scale. The current reverse twist does not INCREASE term premium beyond its natural level — it prevents the Fed from SUPPRESSING it. The counterfactual: IF the Fed were reinvesting MBS into coupons (as it did pre-QT), term premium would be ~30-50bps lower [LLM-E].

### 3.3 Driver 3 — Japan Demand Retreat and JGB Competition

**T-account chain:**

BOJ rate normalization → JGB 10y yield → 2.3% [WEB-2026-05-24] → hedged UST return < JGB yield [LLM-E] → Japanese life insurers reduce UST purchases → US marginal buyer shifts from price-insensitive foreign CB to price-sensitive domestic fund → required clearing yield ↑

**Quantification:**
- Japan UST holdings: $1.24T → $1.19T (March 2026) [WEB-2026-05-24]
- Q1 2026 selling: **$29.6B** — largest quarterly reduction in ~4 years [WEB-2026-05-24]
- Hedging math: 10y UST 4.56% minus ~250bps hedge cost = ~2.06% net [LLM-E] vs JGB at 2.3% → negative carry to hold UST hedged

**Mechanism:** Japanese life insurers and pension funds historically provided a non-price-sensitive buffer for US long-end demand. As JGB yields rise above hedged UST returns, this demand pool shrinks. Each $100B decline in structural foreign demand requires domestic price-sensitive buyers to absorb more, pushing yields up by an estimated ~15-25bps at the margin [LLM-E — no authoritative empirical estimate available, TRUE_GAP].

**Forward path:** IF BOJ hikes to 1.0-1.5% (which many expect by 2028), THEN JGB 10y could reach 2.5-3.0% → USD/JPY hedging costs decline (as USD-JPY interest rate differential narrows) → the sign of incentive reverses. This is a future tailwind for UST demand, not a current one.

### 3.4 Driver 4 — Inflation Uncertainty Premium

**Mechanism:** Long-dated bond investors require compensation for uncertainty about future inflation over the bond's life. Higher inflation uncertainty → higher term premium. The tariff shock of 2026 creates genuine uncertainty about whether inflation normalizes in 2027 or remains structurally elevated.

**Evidence:**
- 5y5y forward inflation at 2.29% vs 2.0% target → 29bps uncertainty premium [WEB-2026-05-18]
- Core PCE at 3.2% in March → tariff pass-through clearly visible [from fed_framework_2026 research]
- Ferrante Capital (2026): "The term premium story is not being driven by runaway inflation expectations" [WEB-2026-05-24] — inflation is above target but not spiraling; the premium is for uncertainty, not embedded high inflation

**The Volcker credibility analogy (inverted):** In the early 1980s, the Fed's credibility deficit kept term premium elevated until the Volcker disinflation proved durable. Today, the Fed's credibility is intact (5y5y at 2.29%, not 4%), but the tariff shock is creating a temporary "credibility test" — can the Fed durably return inflation to 2.0% against a supply-driven shock without sacrificing growth? The longer core PCE stays above 3.0%, the more this question will build into term premium.

## LENS 4 — INTERACTIONS AND CONNECTIONS

### 4.1 Connection to Fed Framework: Two Instruments, One Economy

The Fed's rate path (3.50-3.75% hold) and the Treasury curve are not independent. Three interaction channels:

**Channel 1: Fiscal channel.** IF higher long-end yields raise government debt servicing costs → fiscal deficit widens further → more coupon issuance required → long-end yields rise further → self-reinforcing loop [LLM-E]. The US federal interest expense is already ~$850-950B/year; each 25bp rise in average coupon rate adds ~$70B annually (on $28T outstanding) [LLM-E].

**Channel 2: Private sector credit channel.** 10y Treasury is the benchmark for mortgages and corporate bonds. 10y at 4.56% → 30y mortgage rate ~7.0-7.3% [LLM-E]. This constrains housing, which constrains consumer wealth effects, which feeds back into lower aggregate demand — a tightening force that operates regardless of the FFR level.

**Channel 3: Rate policy and curve slope.** IF the Fed cuts FFR, THEN 2y rallies → 2s10s steepens → long-end may not move (or may rise if cut is seen as premature on inflation). A bull steepener scenario (both ends rally but short end faster) would only materialize if: (a) inflation breaks convincingly below 2.5% and (b) the fiscal outlook improves. Neither is currently true.

### 4.2 Connection to Balance Sheet Operations (RMPs)

From the prior fed_framework_2026 analysis: RMPs buy T-bills at $10B/month and do not constitute monetary easing at the long end. The connection to the yield curve:

- RMPs keep reserves ample (→ no liquidity pressure that spills into repo → no basis widening that raises long yields via plumbing)
- RMPs do NOT buy coupons → do not provide demand at the 10-30y point → cannot directly compress term premium
- The MBS passive runoff (prepayments → T-bills) slightly increases duration supply to private market → mild upward pressure on term premium [LLM-E]

Net conclusion: Current Fed operations are yield-curve neutral to mildly bearish for long end. [LLM-E]

### 4.3 Swap Spread Implications

Long-end swap spreads have been negative (10y swap rate < 10y Treasury rate) since approximately 2016, driven by SLR/LCR constraints making it cheaper for dealers to receive fixed in swaps than to short Treasuries. [RAW-WIKI Swap_Spreads_Balance_Sheet_Plumbing_Frictions]

With 10y Treasury at 4.56% and 10y USD swap typically ~15-25bps negative spread → 10y USD swap rate approximately **4.31-4.41%** [LLM-E]. This makes swaps cheaper than equivalent-duration Treasuries for fixed-income buyers who face SLR constraints (banks prefer swaps) — a demand bifurcation that creates arbitrage for those without SLR (hedge funds, unregulated buyers can play the spread).

## LENS 5 — SYNTHESIS, RV IMPLICATIONS, AND TRUE_GAPS

### 5.1 Synthesis

The US Treasury yield curve in May 2026 is best described as: **a fiscally-determined bear steepener where the long end has been repriced upward by structural supply/demand imbalance, while the short end remains Fed-anchored in a hold stance.**

Key equilibrium conditions:

| Condition | Status | Impact on Curve |
|---|---|---|
| Fed holds at 3.50-3.75% | ACTIVE | Short end anchored; 2y at 4.13% |
| $1.9T deficit → coupon supply | ACTIVE | Long-end supply premium; 30y at 5.07% |
| Japan selling ($29.6B Q1) | ACTIVE | Marginal bid removed; long-end yield higher |
| Fed not buying coupons | ACTIVE | No term premium suppression |
| Inflation above target (PCE 3.2%) | ACTIVE | Uncertainty premium in breakevens |
| Term premium at 0.68% (ACM, April) | ACTIVE | Up from near-zero in 2021; structural regime shift |

**The dominant force is fiscal supply + term premium normalization.** Inflation expectations are above target but not spiraling — inflation uncertainty is secondary. Japan selling is incremental, not the primary driver. The Fed's hold stance is necessary but not sufficient to keep the curve inverted or flat.

**Trade 1 — Long 2s10s steepener:**

Carry approximately zero (2y carry at 4.13% vs 10y at 4.56% on DV01-neutral position). Risk: inflation spike that triggers long-end bear move before any Fed cut.

**Trade 2 — Long 20y, short 10s/30s butterfly:**
20y estimated ~4.95% [LLM-E] trades ~10-15bps cheap to the 10s/30s fly — structural cheapness from supply overhang since 2020 reintroduction. IF issuance pace holds and structural buyers (insurance, pension) absorb, THEN 20y fly richens 10-15bps [LLM-E]. Duration-neutral. No carry cost if butterfly is balanced.

**Trade 3 — Long 5y TIPS (breakeven widener):**
IF tariff inflation proves more persistent than staff baseline (probability medium), THEN 5y TIPS breakeven rises from ~2.3% toward 2.5-2.7% → long 5y TIPS vs short nominal 5y expresses this view [LLM-E]. The trade benefits if breakevens widen (inflation surprise) and loses if inflation falls rapidly (Scenario A).

**Trade 4 — Outright long 2y (positive carry):**
2y at 4.13% yields 63bps above the overnight rate (3.50%) — positive carry for a funded position [LLM-E]. IF inflation breaks to 2.5% or below in Q2-Q3 2026, THEN 2y rallies 40-60bps on cut expectations materializing. Risk: no cuts all year; limited P&L beyond the carry.

**The false trade — long 30y for duration return:**
With 30y at 5.07%, the nominal yield is attractive but: (a) real yield ~2.5-2.7% [LLM-E] is near historic highs, implying some compression likely; (b) BUT supply premium from FY2027-28 issuance concerns keeps long end vulnerable to further bear moves. 30y is a value trap if fiscal supply worsens. The RV case for the 10y (versus 30y) is stronger: 10y has less exposure to the tail supply risk while capturing most of the rate return.
