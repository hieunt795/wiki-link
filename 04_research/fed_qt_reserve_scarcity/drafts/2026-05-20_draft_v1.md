## Executive Summary

The Federal Reserve's Quantitative Tightening is entering a structurally different phase. The ON RRP buffer — which absorbed ~$2.5T of the reserve drain through 2022-2024 — is now largely exhausted. From this point forward, each dollar of QT translates more directly into a dollar reduction in bank reserves. The structural floor for reserve demand, estimated at ~$3T due to post-GFC payment system changes, constrains how much further QT can run. Four observable signals — SOFR-IORB spread, payment timing delays, SRF usage, and ON RRP balance — form the real-time monitoring dashboard. The Fed's toolkit includes TOMOs (short-term), RMPs (permanent), and structural reforms (LSM for Fedwire), with slowing QT pace as the first-response lever.

## 1. The Two-Phase Structure of QT Reserve Drain

**Phase 1 (2022–2024): Buffered drain**

QT began June 2022 at a $60B/month redemption cap. System reserves were ~$3.3T at peak. During this phase, the ON RRP facility absorbed the shock: as QT reduced Fed assets, the parallel drain of $2.5T in ON RRP balances released pre-existing liquidity into the banking system.

The reserve supply identity clarifies the mechanism:

```
ΔReserves = ΔFed_Assets − ΔTGA − ΔON_RRP − ΔCurrency
```

With ON RRP falling by ~$2.5T between December 2022 and end-2024, reserves remained broadly stable near $3.3T even as the Fed balance sheet shrank significantly. [RAW-BOOK Reserve Floor node; RAW-BOOK QT node]

**Phase 2 (2025–): Direct drain**

By late 2024, ON RRP balances fell below $100B — the buffer is effectively exhausted. TGA dynamics (debt ceiling mechanics, spending volatility) remain a wildcard, but the ON RRP "shock absorber" is gone. Each additional $100B of QT now reduces bank reserves by approximately $80-100B net. [LLM based on reserve supply identity]

## 2. Why the Reserve Floor is ~$3T, Not ~$10B

The pre-GFC federal funds system functioned with just $10B in system reserves. Three post-GFC structural changes raised the effective minimum to ~$3T: [RAW-BOOK Reserve Floor node, citing Duffie BPEA 2026]

**a) Daylight overdraft stigma.** GSIBs now self-fund intraday liquidity from their own reserves rather than using Fed daylight overdrafts. Pre-GFC, large banks collectively used $120B/day in overdrafts; post-GFC, that fell to <$5B. Each bank must pre-fund its own payment obligations rather than relying on the Fed as intraday lender.

**b) IORB remuneration removes lending incentive.** Pre-GFC, reserves earned 0% — banks actively lent excess reserves overnight to earn a return. Post-GFC, IORB ≈ market rate — reserves are a competitive investment asset. Banks (notably JPMorgan, whose reserves fell from $409B to $63B in 2023-25) treat reserves as one investment among many, rotating into higher-yielding Treasuries when the spread justifies it.

**c) Frictional interbank market.** FDIC insurance fees (up to 55bps combined) make interbank reserve lending expensive for the receiving bank. Reserve redistribution through the interbank market is no longer frictionless.

Together, these create a **reserve demand ratchet**: each period of abundant reserves allows banks to run leaner liquidity management operations. When reserves tighten, the system cannot easily revert to pre-GFC efficiency. [RAW-BOOK Reserve Floor node, citing Acharya-Rajan 2022]

## 3. Repo Market Signals: The Real-Time Dashboard

Approaching reserve scarcity manifests progressively across four indicators, from leading to lagging:

| Signal | Normal | Elevated | Stressed |
|--------|--------|----------|---------|
| SOFR-IORB spread | −5bps to 0bps | 10-15bps | >25bps |
| ON RRP balance | Any level | <$100B (buffer gone) | <$25B |
| SRF usage | Zero | Any non-trivial | Large scale |
| Payment delays | Low | Rising | Record high |

**Canonical calibration — September 17, 2019:**  
System reserves hit ~$1.4T; SOFR spiked to IORB + 315bps; interdealer rates briefly 1000bps above IORB. Payment timing delays were simultaneously at their 10-year high. This was not a simple reserve shortage — it was a *distribution* failure: JPMorgan held reserves but did not lend them due to LCR/regulatory constraints. [RAW-BOOK Reserve Floor node]

**Key insight:** Aggregate reserve level is necessary but not sufficient. The *distribution* of reserves across dealer banks matters. With post-GFC LCR/SLR constraints, large banks may hoard reserves even when system-wide aggregates appear ample.

**Monitoring caveat:** Quarter-end SOFR spikes are mechanically driven by FBO window-dressing (balance sheet compression for regulatory reporting) — not structural scarcity. These are $200-400B temporary drains that revert within 1-2 days. [RAW-BOOK Reserve Floor node]

## 4. SLR Interaction: The Hidden Amplifier

Dealer balance sheet constraints (SLR — Supplementary Leverage Ratio) interact with reserve scarcity in a non-linear way:

```
Reserves approach floor
    ↓
Dealers approach SLR limit (Treasury holdings + repo)
    ↓
Dealers cannot expand balance sheet to intermediate repo
    ↓
Localized SOFR spike even with "ample" aggregate reserves
    ↓
Appears as scarcity even when aggregate level is comfortable
```

[RAW-BOOK SLR LCR node; RAW-BOOK Reserve Floor node]

The 2020-21 SLR exemption for Treasuries materially eased this friction by allowing dealers to expand intermediation capacity without hitting the leverage limit. Its expiration in March 2021 contributed to balance sheet pressure. The Basel III Endgame SLR rules represent a structural headwind to dealer intermediation capacity going forward. ## 5. Fed Policy Toolkit

Four tools, in order of implementation speed:

**Tool 1 — Slow/Stop QT** (immediate)  
The primary lever. The Fed has signaled it monitors SOFR-IORB spread as a key indicator. The Feb 2024 pace reduction (from $60B to $25B/month) demonstrated willingness to slow before reaching "scarce" territory. [RAW-BOOK QT node]

**Tool 2 — TOMOs** (days)  
Temporary repo operations inject reserves without permanent balance sheet expansion. Duffie recommends more systematic TOMOs to smooth period-end volatility, reducing the required permanent reserve buffer. [RAW-BOOK Reserve Floor node]

**Tool 3 — Reserve Management Purchases (RMPs)** (weeks)  
T-bill purchases permanently replenish reserves without targeting long-term yields. No "stealth QE" signal risk. Used post-2019 repocalypse at ~$60B/month pace.

**Tool 4 — Fedwire LSM** (multi-year)  
Structural: a liquidity savings mechanism for Fedwire would offset outgoing payments against incoming flows before settlement, reducing opening balance requirements by potentially $200-500B. [RAW-BOOK Reserve Floor node; LLM-E for magnitude]

## 6. Conclusions

1. **The QT buffer is gone.** ON RRP depletion means the Fed is now in the direct-drain phase. Remaining QT runway is limited — estimated $200-400B before approaching the structural floor. [LLM-E]

2. **The floor is structural, not cyclical.** Post-GFC payment system changes (RTGS pre-funding + IORB + FDIC fees) have permanently raised minimum reserve demand. The Fed cannot rely on pre-GFC experience to calibrate QT end-point.

3. **Distribution matters as much as level.** Sep 2019 shows that localized reserve maldistribution at key dealers can trigger market stress even with system-wide aggregates appearing adequate. SLR constraints amplify this.

4. **Signal priority:** Monitor SOFR-IORB spread daily. Persistent elevation above 15bps should trigger Fed response. Quarter-end spikes are noise; week-long elevations are signal.

5. **Enabling reforms extend QT runway.** A Fedwire LSM and destigmatized SRF could each lower the structural floor by $200-500B, meaningfully extending how far QT can run — but neither is near-term.
