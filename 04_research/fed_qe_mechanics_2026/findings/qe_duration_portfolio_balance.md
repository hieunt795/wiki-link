## Finding

QE functions as a massive duration-for-liquidity swap. By purchasing long-duration government bonds and issuing near-zero-duration reserves, the central bank systematically "strips" or "extracts" interest rate risk (duration) from the private sector.

### Duration Extraction
- The Fed buys 10-year or 30-year Treasuries (high duration) and pays with reserves (overnight duration).
- This reduces the total amount of duration held by private investors, which theoretically lowers the "term premium" and long-term yields [RAW-CLIP Japan II].

### Portfolio Rebalancing Effect
- Investors who sell their bonds to the Fed (or whose bonds are displaced) now hold cash (deposits/reserves) which has zero yield and zero duration.
- To meet their duration or yield targets, these institutional investors (pension funds, insurers) seek to buy other risky assets (corporate bonds, equities, private credit), pushing up their prices and further easing financial conditions [RAW-BOOK Bindseil L3121, RAW-BOOK Singh Ch.4].

## Evidence Chain
1. Fed buys long-term bonds → 2. Supply of duration in private hands falls → 3. Investors have excess cash → 4. Reinvestment in other risky assets lowers spreads and yields across the curve [LLM].

## Limitations / Caveats
The effectiveness of the portfolio balance channel is contested and may diminish once yields reach the zero lower bound or if investors are highly risk-averse [RAW-BOOK Bindseil].
